# October 5 feedback — local implementation

This change addresses the attached Euchre notes. It has not been committed, pushed, or deployed.

## Recovery and history

- Human turns wait indefinitely. Legacy nudge/abandon messages cannot play a human card or end the match.
- Opening a saved table no longer eagerly resolves the actor during page rendering. Connection failures leave a reconnect button and saved-games link.
- An uncertain move acknowledgement triggers an authoritative snapshot before reporting a retry. The client never blindly resends a move that may have committed.
- Same-browser bookmarks retain twelve recently visited tables, including links whose snapshots are temporarily unavailable. Bookmarks grant no access: each table still verifies the signed guest's ownership.
- New matches write to the selected email profile when one exists. Matches completed under the original signed browser identity remain separately accessible in Your Games.
- Completed table actors are retained for thirty days after their durable match record exists, rather than ten minutes. Older summary records remain in the profile.

## Playing the game

- A new-table screen offers stick-the-dealer and natural-trump requirements. Both are off by default for web-created games. The natural-trump rule counts the printed suit, so a left bower alone cannot qualify; a picking-up dealer can count the up-card. A stuck dealer is exempt to prevent a dead end, explained before starting.
- The authoritative reducer enforces these rules and follow-suit for every seat, including AI seats. Jev remains the decision model; this change does not substitute a conversational model for decisions.
- The board and hand share a continuous full-width felt surface. Desktop hand controls align with the larger cards, and the led-suit badge sits beside trump without overlapping the partner. Ordering up sends the visible up-card to the dealer with a reduced-motion-aware transition; reconnecting does not replay it.
- Desktop seats surround the cards, with player silhouettes, caller/dealer/lead indicators and prominent trump and numeric scores, accompanied by six/four counters. Phones use a compact layout with independent, non-overlapping card targets.
- Short portrait phones use one hand row to preserve room for readable played cards. Taller phones retain the two-row hand; landscape phones and laptops use the available width. Select-and-confirm and double-click play remain supported.
- AI bidding pauses are longer, and passes stay visible by seat. A paused winning trick explicitly says Continue must precede the next lead.
- Review tricks opens the current public trick history or an earlier archived hand. Buried and sitting-out cards use a separate post-hand contract, never the live view or AI prompt. Older hands predating this version cannot be reconstructed through this archive.
- Settings can remain open, be pinned and moved with pointer or arrow keys. The pinned panel is bounded to the viewport.
- Enabling sound plays a test cue. Trump, hand results and game wins now invoke sound cues; lone calls get an announcement, and a four-point loner gets a celebration respecting reduced-motion preferences.
- Generated banter screens out table coaching; old partner nudges no longer fire. Navigation and copy use direct language and American spelling.

## Validation and limits

- Local Svelte/type check, server tests, gateway tests and production build are run before handoff.
- Session HTTP checks exercise owner 200, other browser 403, anonymous 401, foreign origin 403 and unavailable table 503.
- Browser checks cover setup, card selection, double-click, follow-suit restrictions, a five-trick hand, paused Continue, reconnect and post-hand archive retrieval at laptop/phone sizes.
- The shuffle is seeded Fisher–Yates. In a deterministic sample of 10,000 five-card hands, 69.30% lacked at least one suit; exact deck math gives 69.51%. This is a sanity check, not proof of statistical or cryptographic fairness. Cut ordering and shuffle determinism are already covered by reducer tests.
- Read-only production logs contain missing-actor errors. They do not establish the exact cause of the reported third-trick HTTP 500s. Actors already destroyed by the old policy cannot be recreated from bookmarks; the local changes prevent several identified failure paths and preserve future tables.
- Emulator/browser viewport checks do not establish physical iOS/Android audio behavior. Physical device acceptance and production rollout remain separate.
